# Editorial grammar

Use this reference to convert a transcript and a performance into a deliberate
edit. The goal is not constant spectacle. The goal is continuous, meaningful
visual guidance.

## Build the beat sheet

Split speech at semantic boundaries, not fixed time intervals. For every beat,
record:

| Field | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Spoken interval | Which exact words and word timings define the beat? |
| Function | Hook, claim, explanation, proof, objection, contrast, list, payoff, or CTA? |
| Emotion | Confidence, risk, relief, doubt, urgency, curiosity, or trust? |
| Visual role | Rail, emphasis, hero, proof, connector, or drop? |
| Occupancy | Where are the face, hands, microphone, props, and free background? |
| Motion verb | Snap, stack, count, draw, push, erase, lift, isolate, connect, or reveal? |
| Handoff | What causes the next visual state to begin? |

Use one sentence to state the visual argument of a sequence. If that sentence
cannot be written, the graphics probably do not yet have a job.

## Caption hierarchy

### Rail

The rail carries most verbatim speech. It should be readable, restrained, and
stable enough to orient the viewer.

- Default to 2-6 words per page.
- Keep one or two visual lines.
- Break on phrases, not arbitrary character counts.
- Use exact token timing for the active-word state.
- Prefer a stable baseline and consistent safe-zone over a constantly moving
  caption block.
- Use contrast, weight, color, or a small positional accent for active words.

### Emphasis

Promote a word only when its meaning earns a visual change.

- Negation: readable word first, then a selective strike, erase, or rejection.
- Quantity: count, scale, tabular alignment, or a chart-like build.
- Contrast: separate positions or opposing motion directions.
- Process: ordered stack, path, connector, or progressive reveal.
- Certainty: fast snap into a locked resting pose.
- Doubt: softer drift or unstable alignment, followed by resolution.

Do not apply the same emphasis to every word. If everything is accented, there
is no hierarchy.

### Hero

A hero is a scarce, high-attention event. Use no more than one promoted hero per
thought block.

- It may become full-screen only after an earned setup.
- It must use the current spoken words or a clearly supporting proof artifact.
- It must preserve transcript continuity.
- It needs a readable resting pose before exit.
- It must hand back to the speaker or next proof beat cleanly.

## Line, row, and word animation selection

Use the smallest animation unit that communicates the idea.

| Unit | Best use | Failure mode |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Whole block | Short title, stable label, calm statement | Repeating it on every caption feels mechanical |
| Row | Contrast pairs, ordered clauses, two-part escalation | More than two rows becomes a presentation slide |
| Word | Active captions, lists, semantic emphasis | Uncontrolled stagger becomes visual noise |
| Letter | Logos, one short hero word, spelling-specific idea | Long copy becomes unreadable and slow |

For per-word entry, each word begins from its own spoken timing. A visual
pre-roll may begin before the audio token only when it does not overlap the
previous word or reveal the word prematurely. The resting state should occupy
more time than the transition state.

## Opening rules

- Start on the actual performance unless a cold-open card is explicitly part
  of the creative direction.
- Preserve at least the first 0.3-0.8 seconds before a covering transition.
- Use the first meaningful word, gesture, or look as the hook trigger.
- If a title appears immediately, integrate it around the subject or in depth
  rather than placing an opaque card over the person.

## Full-screen rules

A full-screen interstitial is justified when it creates proof, scale, clarity,
or a purposeful tonal break. It is not justified merely because the editor can
make one.

- Reveal the spoken copy as it is said; do not preload the paragraph.
- Keep ordinary copy to two lines.
- Use one focal hierarchy: one dominant phrase plus at most one supporting
  line or proof mark.
- Return before the speaker's performance becomes emotionally relevant again.
- Keep captions visible inside the designed screen, or make the designed words
  themselves the caption.

## Pacing and variation

Build change at several intensities:

- micro: active word, highlight, icon tick, underline, or subtle crop;
- medium: punch-in, new caption composition, annotation, split, or diagram
  stage;
- major: hard cut, full-screen proof, B-roll, scene change, or depth reveal.

Avoid 3-8 second regions in which only identical caption pages change. During a
busy short-form passage, aim for a perceptible change every 0.4-1.5 seconds,
but alternate micro, medium, and major events so the viewer can still read.

A useful distribution is:

- 60-75% coherent primary rail;
- 15-30% semantic emphasis variants;
- 5-15% hero, proof, or depth events.

The distribution is a diagnostic, not a quota.

## Composition and face protection

- Detect the face and protect eyes and mouth first.
- Leave at least 30% of the face visible in every rolling 300ms window.
- Treat hands, a held product, and the microphone as meaningful foreground,
  not empty space.
- Anchor graphics to real planes: wall, desk, window, negative space, or
  behind-subject depth.
- Do not use a centered opaque card when the photographed scene supplies a
  better spatial relationship.
- Inspect at least three poses in every animated section; a safe position in
  one frame can cover a moving face in the next.

## Semantic visual selection

| Spoken idea | Candidate visual |
| --- | --- |
| People or identity | Subject isolate, portrait, labels, relationship lines |
| Place | Map, route, coordinate, location word integrated with footage |
| Number or outcome | Count-up, chart, large tabular figure, proof screenshot |
| Process | Pipeline, stack, path, nodes, sequential build |
| Comparison | Split field, mirrored columns, opposing positions |
| Trust | Testimonial, result capture, credential, source, product view |
| Rejection | Terms enter separately; only the rejected term is crossed or removed |
| Transformation | Foreground/background depth, wipe, reframing, state morph |

When presenting a list such as "liar, fraud, nonsense", introduce each term at
a separate stable position and time. Let the audience read each one. Apply a
strike or rejection only to the intended term and keep the mark local to that
word.

## Sound grammar

- Dialogue is the master.
- Use a cue for a meaningful impact, reveal, or transition, not for every
  animated word.
- Keep SFX short and quieter than consonants.
- Use music energy to support the narrative arc; do not cut speech merely to
  satisfy a generic beat grid.
- Review with headphones, speakers, and dialogue soloed when possible.
