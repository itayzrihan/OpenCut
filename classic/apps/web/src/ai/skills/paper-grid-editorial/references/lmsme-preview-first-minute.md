## Style model learned from all 180 frames

1. **Speech drives layout.** Words become lists, equations, maps, diagrams,
   numbers, and chapter hinges.
2. **The speaker remains a recurring anchor.** Full-screen graphics are
   interleaved with performance rather than replacing it for long stretches.
3. **Copy builds in time.** Lists and clauses rarely appear completed on their
   first frame.
4. **Most compositions use one focal idea.** Supporting copy is smaller and
   subordinate.
5. **Visual modes rotate.** Speaker, kinetic type, map, stat, process diagram,
   website proof, icon system, depth/avatar, and dark viewer-focus scenes all
   occur within one minute.
6. **Repetition is local.** Related list items reuse a grammar, but the next
   thought block changes grammar.
7. **Color is semantic.** Green marks benefit/result/trust; red marks risk,
   negation, or high-friction identity.
8. **Transitions hand off concepts.** A graph reveals footage, an equation
   collapses into business, and reserved space receives "DROPPED".

The lesson is not to copy this video's white cards, fonts, or exact pacing. It
is to make every visual transformation answer the currently spoken idea.
